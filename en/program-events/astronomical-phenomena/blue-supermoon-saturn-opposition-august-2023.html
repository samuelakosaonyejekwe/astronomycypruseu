    Blue Supermoon and Opposition of Saturn (EN)

    At the end of August, an extraordinary event awaits observers on Earth.

    When going around the Sun our planet approximately once a year occupies a position between the slow Saturn and the Sun.

    When this happens, astronomers say that Saturn is at opposition. At this time, Saturn is closest to the Earth.

    And such an opposition will take place on August 27, when there will be 1.3 billion km to the ringed planet.

    Due to the fact that the cycle of the lunar phases is somewhat shorter than one month and the previous full Moon was on August 1, a second full Moon will occur on August 31. This second full moon of the Moon is called a Blue Moon because it rarely occurs.

    But what makes this full Moon even rarer is the location of our satellite in its orbit. Due to its movement in an elongated orbit, the Moon is either approaching or moving away from the Earth. On August 31, the Moon will be closer than usual.

    Such full Moons are called Supermoons.

    On the last day of the month, at sunset, Saturn and the Full Moon will rise from the opposite side, both closer than usual and looking brighter than usual.

    The KITION PLANETARIUM & OBSERVATORY invites you on August 31 to a program dedicated to this extraordinary gift from the heavens;

    Blue Supermoon and Saturn Opposition. At the end of the program, weather permitting, there will be observations of Saturn and its fascinating rings, as well as the Supermoon with the large telescopes of our observatory.

    The Moon will appear not only full, but also brighter and larger than usual.

    The session starts in Russian language at 19:30, in Greek at 20:30 and in English at 21:30.

    The ticket costs 10 euros. Seats are limited and tickets are available online only.
